# Breakdown: See the Physics. For teachers

Breakdown is a pool game for iPhone, iPad and Mac whose table shows its own physics. Every shot is worked out by the
game, step by step, and the Physics Lens replays it slowly with a short sentence at each moment that matters. This
page says which parts of a school physics course the game shows, and where to find each one.

## What it shows

- **Momentum handed from ball to ball.** In a straight hit the white ball stops and the other ball leaves with its
  speed. The Lens explains the hit as it happens ("Energy handed off"), with the speeds before and after.
- **Friction turning a slide into a roll.** A struck ball first skids, then the cloth's friction spins it up until it
  rolls. The Lens draws a sliding ball's path dashed and a rolling ball's path solid, and says when and where the skid
  turns into a roll ("Skid turns into roll").
- **Energy lost at each hit.** Each hit between balls and each bounce off a cushion keeps only part of the energy; the
  Lens gives the speed in and the speed out ("The cushion pushes back").
- **Spin.** Striking above or below the centre gives topspin or backspin, which carries the white ball on or pulls it
  back after the hit. Striking to one side bends its path while it slides ("The path bends").
- **A ball in flight.** A steeply raised cue drives the ball down into the slate, which throws it up; the Lens follows
  the flight and the landing ("Off the slate", "Lands").
- **The same shot on ice or on the Moon.** The Surface button plays the very same shot again on another surface. On
  ice there is almost no grip, so balls skid far before they roll; on the Moon gravity is a sixth of Earth's, so
  everything slides and rolls six times farther.

## Lessons and explanations by topic

The six lessons are under Learn in the menu. Each is a few minutes long and ends with a trick shot built on the same
idea.

| Topic | Lesson | Lens explanation to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Momentum in a head-on hit | 1. Stop shot | Energy handed off |
| Friction and rolling | 2. Follow, 3. Draw | Skid turns into roll |
| Angles and friction at contact | 4. Cut and throw | Throw |
| Spin and a curved path | 5. Curve | The path bends |
| Flight and landing | 6. Jump | Off the slate, Lands |
| Energy lost at a bounce | any shot off a cushion | The cushion pushes back |
| Gravity and grip | any shot, then Surface: Ice or Moon | Skid turns into roll |

Practice, in the menu, is a free table for trying a shot again with one thing changed. Try again puts the balls back
where they were, and the last try stays on the table to compare.

## Numbers and equations

Every figure on screen is in metric units (metres per second, newtons, joules, degrees, millimetres) and is worked out
by the game, not drawn by hand. Before a shot, Your strike shows the strike about to be made: its speed, its push, the
force over the tip's contact, its energy and its spin; after the shot it shows what was planned beside what the stroke
delivered. Turn on Show the maths in Options and every explanation in the Lens carries the equation behind it.

## Privacy and the classroom

The game has no account of its own, no advertising and no purchases. It collects nothing, and it plays with no
internet connection at all. The one optional extra is the daily board: a player who chooses to sign in to it has
their daily score carried by the device's own game service. For a class, turn on **Hide the board** in Options: the
game then never signs in and shows no board, while today's shot, its score and the streak still work. The privacy
policy says this in full.

The game can also be played with VoiceOver, with a keyboard or a game controller alone, with larger text, with
Reduce Motion, and with the controls on the left for a left hand (Controls on the left in Options).
