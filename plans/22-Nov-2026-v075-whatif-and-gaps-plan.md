# Breakdown 0.7.5: what if, and a game that works everywhere

1. RELEASE SUMMARY

Breakdown 0.7.5 builds the what-if replay. After any shot of your own in free play, or a judged try at a trick shot, a lesson or a Behind the 8-Ball situation, "What if" lets you nudge one setting (the aim, the power, the follow or draw, the side, or the cue angle on a raised shot) and see the other future: its paths drawn in violet beside the real ones, ghost balls that roll with the real ones when you play both, a marker where the two futures part, and one plain paragraph saying what changed, what happened differently at the hit, and how a tenth of a degree at the cue became forty centimetres at the finish. "Keep this one" turns it into your next try, and shooting without touching anything plays exactly the future you saw. Around it, 0.7.5 fixes every verified bug: the first minute loses its bouncing equipment card and the physics constants on the big screens; Practice always racks the pool table; menus, keys and the controller stop acting behind the menu; a match calls you "You", ends with "You win" instead of "Keep shooting", stays quiet when the computer wins, and says what the computer chose; the other tables stop talking about cue balls, pockets and the 9 ball; the Lens replays keep their promises (held moments you can continue on every screen, an Auto speed, the intro's end card, Ride restored); the raised-cue trick shots are authored on angles the control can choose and the judge tells you to raise the cue; and the notebook, the side column, the Mac and the open Duo stop covering, clipping or shrinking the table.

2. FEATURES

W. The what-if replay (REQUIRED; spec 4.3)
- Player sees: a "What if" button (branch symbol) in the playback row beside Replay, shown only when the balls are at rest after your own shot in free play or a judged try in a trick shot, lesson or Behind the 8-Ball (never on a break, never in a match, never in the first minute, never during the computer's turn). Tapping it opens one card in the card slot (on the phone below the table, in the notebook on iPad, Mac and the open Duo, so nothing covers the table) with Aim, Power, Spin and, for a raised cue, Cue angle; each moves in clicks with minus and plus (left/right, softer/harder, lower/higher, left/right, one menu angle down/up) and shows the offset ("0.25° left", "10% harder", "2.5 mm lower"). Within moments the other future appears in violet: the alternate paths (dotted, so they read without colour), ghost balls, the other cue ball's resting ring labelled "What if", and a diamond where the futures part, with a matching diamond on the scrub bar. "Play both" replays the shot with the ghosts rolling beside the real balls; "Where they part" jumps there. The card says, in at most four sentences: the change ("This version is aimed 0.25 degrees further left."), what happened differently at the hit ("At the hit, the 3 leaves 1.2 degrees further left."), the outcome ("This time the 3 drops in the corner pocket.", or "Same result: the shot has room for a change this size."), and, when the gap grew more than tenfold, "At the first hit the two cue balls were 3 millimetres apart; they stop 40 centimetres apart." In a trick shot it adds "This version makes the shot." or "This version still misses." under a fixed line "Same stroke, one setting changed." "Keep this one" (free play, trick shots from the trick's start, lessons when every nudged control is open, Behind the 8-Ball) arms a retry with those settings; Shoot untouched plays exactly that future, and the usual retry card then names the change. Nothing plays by itself; nothing is judged or marked done by a what-if.
- Award: the spec's biggest unbuilt idea, and the strongest physics lesson the game can give: sensitivity, seen on the real solver with the same seed and stroke, one setting at a time.
- Files: see stream whatif (new WhatIfFork, WhatIfViews, WhatIfOverlay, WhatIfWords, package TimelineFork and ShotNudge); hooks in FOUNDATION.

3. DROPPED

- What-if in matches: in a match the recording is replaced by a still table about 1.5 s after the balls stop, and holding the turn for a preview needs holds in the settle beat, the opponent and the clock; free play and the three learn modes are where the lesson lands. A later release can add a view-only match what-if.
- What-if on breaks: every nudge changes the first hit, so the comparison teaches little, and excluding it keeps the first minute untouched.
- Teacher's StrikeBuilder refactor of shootNow and breakNow: a literal move of the path every authored shot depends on, with no compile available to builders; the nudge-the-delivered-input design plus the kept-strike override gives exact reproduction without it.
- Widening playback past the real shot's end (showman's playbackEnd): duration feeds aimReady, which gates every mode, the opponent and the clock; the overlay runs its own tail clock instead.
- Two pinned branches (teacher's A and B), slowing at the parting point, Mac arrow keys and controller control of the what-if: more state and keys for little gain; one alternate, buttons, and a Mac menu item.
- A mode verdict line in lessons and Behind the 8-Ball what-ifs: each has its own judge with locks and calls; trick shots get the line through TrickShotJudge.passes, the others show the outcome only.
- Nine-ball push-out: state, a declaration control, a ruling, a new choice and the computer's push-out play on both sides; the rules plan cut it on purpose. A release of its own. Only its stale "two players on one device" header line is fixed.
- iPad "Play here" carrying the game across windows: moving model ownership out of ShowpieceView touches every window path with no compile available; the hint is reworded to say the other window's game ends.
- Deleting the dead first-launch skip controls, first-break card and menu backdrop: no visible change, and it edits the first-minute state machine in a release that already changes the first minute; only the stale comments are fixed.
- A recap of balls cleared and fouls on the result card: text the end of a game does not need.
- "Pins reset" reason for bar billiards rulings: the pegs standing again is the table's normal state; the Lens caption names what a fallen peg costs instead.
- Always reserving the caption strip's equation line: it would take more height from the table.
- Locking the cue-angle menu while an 8-ball escape rolls: taking the angle from the attempt fixes the judging fully.
- Changing the spoken tip step size: it already reaches the narrowest band.

```swift
import Foundation
import Observation

/// The what-if replay (spec 4.3): one nudged copy of the player's shot, solved beside the real one. Owned by the model
/// (model.whatIf); never loads its recording onto the table, never touches isSolving, never judges or marks anything.
@MainActor @Observable final class WhatIfFork {
    @ObservationIgnored unowned let model: ShowpieceModel
    init(model: ShowpieceModel) { self.model = model }
    /// The card, the nudge controls and the other future are up.
    private(set) var isOpen = false
    /// The entry may be offered now (the button and the Mac menu item read it).
    var canOpen: Bool { false }
    /// Open and still about the recording it was opened on. Every result card steps aside while this is true.
    var isShowing: Bool { false }
    /// "Keep this one" may be offered.
    var canKeep: Bool { false }
    func open() {}
    func close() { isOpen = false }
    func keep() {}
}
```
