Author: Claude Opus 5.5 High (table-opponent builder)

- What: The computer plays bumper pool and five-pin at Easy, Medium and Hard. At bumper pool it banks and ricochets its balls off the bumpers into its cup, or rolls them nearer when no cup shot is likely; at five-pin it drives its ball into yours so yours scatters the pins, or touches yours softly when nothing scores often enough. "Why that shot" explains each stroke in plain words ("Off a bumper on the way.", "It played for the pins. Points on an average stroke of its hand: about 4. Struck perfectly: 6.", "Nothing scored often enough, so it touched your ball softly to give away as little as possible.").
- Why: Bumper pool and five-pin had their rules but nobody to play them against; a computer that plays them gives them a game, and a bumper-pool computer that banks off the bumpers keeps producing physics worth watching.
- How: OpponentPlanner.plan hands bumper pool and five-pin to two new planners before anything pool-specific runs, so eight-ball and nine-ball plan exactly as before. Both share a new search (TableOpponentSearch) that tries strokes all round the ball on the real solver: a fan of aims and speeds, smoothed by the level's own aim wobble, the best ideas refined a tenth of a degree at a time, and the finalists struck again with the level's own hand, the highest average winning. The wobble samples are drawn from seeds mixed out of the stroke's own seed, never that seed itself, so the computer cannot plan around the very wobble it is about to play. Every phase is capped by a count of solves, never by time, so a plan is the same on every device and a saved match replays the same. Bumper pool values its own balls in its cup, the other side's in theirs, fouls, and how much nearer each side's balls lie to their cups; five-pin values the points (hard also keeps its own ball away from the pins). Easy bumper pool plays the raw best of a coarse fan; easy five-pin aims by eye with no solves at all. The aim window "Why that shot" quotes counts the nearby aims that, struck perfectly, still sink one of its balls in its cup with no foul (bumper pool) or still score at least a white pin (five-pin); it is not a bar on the search's value, whose position and leave terms would hide the window of a short cup shot. The computer's think is now cancelled properly when a match is left or restarted. A per-game noise scale exists for tuning a level that proves too strong on one table; every game is at 1.
- Checked: reviewed against the brief by Claude Opus 5.5 High (API names, concurrency, the switch over the plan's kinds, the translations); written without building or running (six builders share one Mac; the coordinator compiles once after the merge). New tests in BreakdownTests/TableOpponentTests.swift; the bumper-pool opening cup shot is a physical claim nobody has run yet and should be checked first. MatchOpponentTests are unchanged and the pool path is untouched. The self-play check (hard against easy, whole games) runs with SELF_PLAY=1 and has not been run, so no table's noise scale has been tuned.
