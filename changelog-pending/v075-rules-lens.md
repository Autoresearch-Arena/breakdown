Author: Claude Opus 5.5 High (stream rules-lens)

- What: Eight-ball now has the behind-the-head-line rule: with ball in hand behind the line, hitting first a ball that also started behind the line, without the cue ball crossing the line before that hit, is a foul (a tie with a ball beyond the line goes to the shooter; a ball on the line counts as behind, as in the placement check). The computer never aims a pot, placement, safety or plain hit at such a ball; when every ball it may hit is behind the line it kicks off the foot cushion, which crosses the line first. Leaving a match while the computer thinks now stops its pool search at the next step instead of letting it run on. Bar billiards never leaves a white or the red standing inside a peg's spot (the red goes back near its own spot, a white to the nearest free place beside where it stopped, also on the last table of a finished game), and once time is up a bed with nothing left to hit ends the game in that shot, with its points counted. Easy five-pin no longer drives its own ball through the castle when the other ball lies beyond it: it looks for a clear full hit, then a thinner hit, then a one-cushion kick. In the Lens, the other tables' captions name balls the way the table does ("A white hits the bumper", "The red drops into the hole", "The marked red..."), never "the cue ball" or "the 3 ball"; a bar-billiards hole is "Into the hole", a bumper-pool cup "Into the cup", a peg knocked over is "Peg down" with what it costs (the black peg the whole score, a white peg the points of this break), and the side-spin title says "the ball" off the pool table. A cue strike on a still table gets no caption. The hit diagram fades out with the camera instead of vanishing when a held hit ends. The five-pin castle has a faint ring round it in the Lens, and bar billiards' pegs are no longer drawn with a red one. Nine-ball's and eight-ball's file headers describe play against the computer, not two people on one device. The pool table's captions are unchanged.
- Why: The verified bug review of 0.7.4 (B2 and B3) and the coordinator's play of 0.7.4 on the iPhone 17 Pro Max simulator (R-A): ball in hand behind the line had no rule, so a scratch on the break gave a free shot at the balls behind the line and the computer played them; an abandoned think ran on; bar-billiards balls could be left inside a peg; a timed game could go on with an empty bed; easy five-pin fouled shots it could see; the Lens called bumper-pool and bar-billiards balls by pool numbers and their cups "pockets"; a zero-speed strike was captioned; the hit diagram popped off; the five-pin castle was hard to find in the Lens.
- How: ShotFacts records where each first-touched ball started, the farthest the cue ball got along the table before its first contact (worked out exactly from the recorded segments, not sampled) and the table's length; EightBallRules places the head line at a quarter of that length (a deliberate restatement of NextShot.headLineX, because the rules module may not import the physics module; a package test pins the two together) and reads the new foul only when the shooter's ball in hand was behind the line. Nine-ball needs no copy: none of its rulings gives ball in hand behind the line. OpponentPlanner gains directTargets, used by the candidates, safeties, placements and easy's look, and a foot-cushion kick in its fallback; it checks Task.isCancelled after its sweep and before its ball-in-hand fallback. BarBilliardsRules.nextLayout moves any ball that blocks a peg (TableSpots.blocksAPin) with TableSpots.nearestFree, after the spare white takes the next striker's place and before the next white is set on the D, and on the game-over return; outcome ends a timed game when the red is out of the game and no white in the game rests on the table. FivePinPlanner's easy fallback checks every line with the castle-distance test and the red's clear-path test. CaptionEngine names balls through TableWords.ballName with the solved table and the striking ball, and chooses the hole, cup, peg and bumper words by table. LensLayer keeps the contact drawing's last pose and fades it from its current opacity over the same quarter second it fades in; a new held hit during that fade carries on from the opacity on screen; it is still cut at once when the Lens leaves, the recording is rebuilt or the hold is not a hit, and none of it is in the Lens fingerprint. The castle ring is a thin annulus at 0.35 opacity in the paper colour, 12 mm outside the outer pin marks. Fourteen new strings, translated in all eleven languages (localization/pending/v075-rules-lens.json).
- Checked: the package's tests (all 232, including the new ones for the head-line readings, the head-line foul and its tie and on-the-line cases, the head line matching NextShot.headLineX, the pegs, the red, the finished game's layout and the empty bed) pass with swift test on the package. App tests written but not run here (the coordinator compiles once after the merge): MatchOpponentTests (no direct shot at a ball behind the line at any level, and the kick crosses the line when nothing else is left), TableOpponentTests (easy five-pin with the other ball beyond the castle picks a line whose legs clear it; the existing from-the-start check is unchanged and should still pass, since the full hit there is clear), CaptionEngineTests (the strike rule's speed gate, a zero-speed strike, the small tables' words with no numbered ball, "pocket" or "the cue ball", the pool table's captions unchanged). The Lens drawing (the fade-out and the castle ring) is not visually checked here. The five-pin pin marks were already 2.6 cm across, over the 1.4 cm R-A asked for, so their size is unchanged; if they still read as specks on the phone, the ring is the part to judge.
