Author: Claude Opus 5.5 High (lens-pacing builder)

- What: Every guided Lens replay of a trick shot, and every replay of a remarkable shot, now stops on the moment the shot is about and shows its equation with the shot's own numbers: the jump's take-off and landing, the end of the massé's curve, the throw, the second rail of a kick, the bank's cushion, the combination's hit, the draw taking hold. Such a replay is short: the strike, the first hit, those moments and little else. Every other replay (not the first launch) now also stops on a jump's take-off, a hit that threw a ball a degree or more, and the end of the biggest bend before the usual moments. Behind it, a new judge reads each shot and says what was remarkable about it: a jump, a curve, a thrown pot, a combination, a kick, a bank, two balls at once, a big draw, a dead stop, a ball too fast for the pocket, a bumper into the cup, the red pin alone, or a break.
- Why: The Lens promises to slow down exactly on the physics, but the massé, the jump and the throw shot could fly past their own point. The judge is what the "worth watching" card (watch-invite stream) and the match card use to offer a shot in the Lens.
- How: PacePlan takes a signature (event numbers) and holds each one as a headline beat, with the plan cut to the signature plus three; the first launch ignores it and stays exactly as before. TrickSignature names each of the eleven shots' moments, and the two new ones'. ShotHighlightJudge holds the rules and the one shared set of event finders both use, and returns only moments that have a caption, so a replay can always hold them. The caption code's bend lookup can now be read from outside (bendEvents); no wording changed. Three rules differ from the plan, each measured on real solves: a dead stop needs 1.0 m/s into the hit, not 1.2 (the Stop Dead answer arrives at 1.14); a curve also needs a raised cue (without it, four in ten ordinary shots counted as curves, from top or back spin bending off a rail); and a pot counts as thrown in only when the throw made it (on the line of centres alone the ball would have missed the hole), because most ordinary cut pots throw about two degrees.
- Checked: every package and app call was read against the real source. The new logic was compiled with the real physics package in a scratch build outside the app and run on every authored trick answer, the player's first break, the showpiece break and about 280 random ordinary shots: each trick shot's own kind is found, every signature moment is held as a headline beat, the first launch's plan is unchanged, and the random shots now show no curves or throws. The new app tests (SignatureBeatTests, ShotHighlightJudgeTests) are written but were not run here; the coordinator compiles and runs them after the merge.
