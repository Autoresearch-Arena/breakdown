Builder: Claude Opus 5.5 High (stream whatif).

- What: The what-if replay. After your own shot in free play, or a judged try at a trick shot, a lesson or a Behind the 8-Ball situation, a "What if" button (a branch symbol) sits beside Replay. It opens one card in the card slot (below the table on a phone, in the notebook on iPad, the Mac and the open Duo) where you move one setting at a time in clicks: the aim (a quarter degree), the power (a twentieth of the bar), follow or draw and side (2.5 millimetres each), and the cue angle on a raised shot (one offered angle). Within moments the other future is drawn in violet beside the real one: dotted paths, hollow ghost balls that roll with the real balls when you play both, rings where its balls stop, a "What if" label at its cue ball, and a diamond where the two futures part (on the table and on the scrub bar). The card says, in a few plain sentences, what changed, what happened differently at the hit, what the result was, and, for an aim or side change, how a few millimetres at the first hit grew into centimetres at the finish; in a trick shot it also says whether this version makes the shot. Play both, Where they part, Reset and Close; Keep this one turns the other future into your next try, and Shoot with nothing touched then plays exactly the future you saw, with the retry card naming the change. Never on a break, in a match, in the first minute or during the computer's turn; nothing plays by itself, and nothing is judged or marked done by a what-if. Also: the speed menu starts with Auto, which hands the pace back to the paced replay; Replay's spoken hint no longer promises slow motion while Ride is on; the scrub bar's impact tick respects Vibration off; the Mac's time readout writes its decimals the way your language does; the last-try contact mark follows the ball you struck on every table; and the spec's status note lists the what-if as built.
- Why: The spec's biggest unbuilt idea (4.3), and the strongest physics lesson the game can give: how sensitive a shot is, seen on the real solver with the same seed and stroke, one setting at a time.
- How: Two package parts: ShotNudge and WhatIfInput (BreakdownPhysics) change a delivered shot in place, a turn by a unit vector, a speed scale, tip offsets with the stroke's own side clamp and a cue angle, so a zero change is the input bit for bit; TimelineFork (BreakdownTimeline) reads two recordings of one shot for the first story difference (ball by ball, phase changes left out, near-simultaneous contacts compared as a set), the first parting along the paths (5 mm, bisected on the clock, so a power change that keeps the line parts only after the contact), the departure at a shared first contact, the gaps at the first hit and at rest, and the changed balls. In the app, WhatIfFork builds the nudged input on the main actor (the same cue pick, launch-speed ratio and clearing raise the model uses, the house cue in the authored modes), solves it through ShowpieceModel.solverCopy on the attempt's own table, cloth and physics generation off the main actor, one solve at a time with an eight-entry cache, and never touches the model's recording, clock, layout, attempts or solving state. Keep goes through the mode's own Try again and then ShowpieceModel.keepFuture, only when the retry was armed from the real attempt. WhatIfWords writes whole localized sentences; WhatIfOverlay draws through the live camera like the last-try ghost; WhatIfViews holds the button and the card. 60 new strings in eleven languages (localization/pending/v075-whatif.json).
- Checked: the package tests (TimelineForkTests: identical recordings, an aim nudge that misses forks at the contact, an aim nudge turns the object ball the other way, a power change parts only after the contact with the gap at the parting point within 0.1 mm of 5 mm, phase changes alone and swapped rack hits are no fork, a pocket against a jaw forks at the pocket, the zero nudge, a turn and its reverse, the side clamp) pass; the whole app typechecks for macOS and the iOS simulator against the package with no new warnings (swiftc -typecheck, no app build); the translation fragment merges into a copy of the catalog with every row accepted and the translation check clean. The app tests (BreakdownTests/WhatIfTests.swift) were written but not run: the coordinator compiles and runs them after the merge. Not covered by a test: the what-if during Watch your best shot and in a match (both refused by canOpen; driving a model into either state needs a cleared table or a match save).
